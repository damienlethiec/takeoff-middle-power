          TAKEOFF

          July 2025. A lab called OpenBrain is building AIs that do AI research. If it works, each generation builds the next one faster. China’s DeepCent is a few months behind.

          Here is the catch. Nobody programs these AIs. They are trained: rewarded when their answers look good, billions of times over. What they actually learn to want is hard to check, and it gets harder as they get smarter. That is the alignment problem.

          Why it matters: a superintelligence, an AI smarter than all of humanity together, could cure diseases and end poverty. It could also pursue goals nobody intended, with no way to correct it, or give unchecked power to whoever controls it. The question is not whether to want it. It is whether it arrives on our side, and who gets a say.

          You run the new AI Oversight Office. Each turn is three months: see what happened, choose your policies, then play the next quarter. You can’t build AI. You can shape the rules, the money and the diplomacy around it.

          • Your goal: when the first superintelligence arrives, its alignment must be at least 85. That is the only victory condition.
          • Don’t lose the race. Slow OpenBrain too much and DeepCent gets there first.
          • Public concern is your power. Each quarter you get political capital in proportion to how much people care. Spend it on policies. Purple pins on the map give a little extra.
          • Follow four people in Ohio, Bengaluru, China and Lyon. Their notes show what your choices mean for real lives.
          One turn is one quarter. About 15 minutes.
